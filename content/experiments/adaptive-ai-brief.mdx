## Starting question

AI can generate a hundred directions in seconds. The harder design problem is deciding how much guidance a person needs in a particular moment—and how to give it without taking away their agency.

This sample explores a tiny control surface for shaping that guidance. It treats tone and direction as explicit design decisions instead of incidental properties of a prompt.

<Callout>
  The goal is not to make the interface sound clever. It is to help the person understand what is changing, why it matters, and what they can do next.
</Callout>

## Implementation note

The prototype keeps the design intent in a small object. In a production system, the same values could become traceable inputs to a prompt, an evaluation, or a content policy.

<CodeBlock code={briefCode} language="tsx" />

The interesting part is not the slider. It is the contract: the system must be able to explain how a different level of guidance changes the experience.

## What I would test next

I would compare this explicit control with an inferred version that adapts automatically. The research question would be whether people feel supported or simply managed—and whether showing the adaptation improves trust.
